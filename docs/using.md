title: Using it

Once your AI app is connected, talk to it the way you would talk to a friend who has the same book open at the same page.
{: .lede}

> **Keep the reader awake.** The assistant talks to KOReader live. The reader needs to be on, connected to Wi-Fi, with the MCP server running and a book open.

## Things to ask

- "What is this book about?"
- "Summarize the chapter so far, without spoilers."
- "Who is this character again?"
- "What does the last paragraph on this page mean?"
- "Find where the letter is first mentioned and take me there."
- "Quiz me on what I read today."
- "What was going on in the world when this was written?"

The assistant decides what to look up: the page you're on, a range of pages, a whole chapter, the table of contents, or a search across the book.

## Point at things by selecting them

Select a word or a passage on the reader, then ask about "this" or "what I selected":

- "What does this word mean here?"
- "Translate what I selected into Spanish."
- "Explain this sentence in simpler terms."

The assistant sees your selection along with the page around it, so you don't have to type any of it out.

## Highlights and notes

The assistant can highlight a passage and attach a note to it. Both are saved in the book the same way as the ones you make on the reader, so they show up in KOReader's highlights list and in anything you export from it.

- "Highlight the sentence about November."
- "Add a note to my selection with the translation."
- "Highlight that and note what we just worked out."

If a passage already has a note, your own writing is safe. Apps that can show a question (Claude can) ask whether to replace your note, add to it, or leave it alone. With other apps, the note is left untouched until you say what to do.

Notes are plain text by default. KOReader 2026.07 and later also display notes written in Markdown, so you can ask for a formatted note.

## It can turn the page

Ask to go somewhere ("take me to chapter 12", "go back to where we started today") and the reader jumps there.

## Your library

The assistant can also see your list of books, your collections and your reading history. That's useful for questions like "What did I read last month?" or "Which of my unread books is closest to this one?"

## Ready-made prompts

Apps that support MCP prompts, such as Claude, offer these as shortcuts:

<div class="table-scroll" markdown="1">

| Prompt | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Explain This | Explains the selected text or the current page |
| Summarize | Summarizes the page, the chapter, or everything you've read so far |
| Let's Discuss | Starts a conversation about themes and ideas |
| Research This | Gives background on the book, the author or the period |
| Take a Note | Helps you write a note and saves it |
| Quiz Me | Asks you questions about what you've read |
| Translate | Translates text into another language |
| Recommend Books | Suggests what to read next |

</div>

## Tips

- **Make a project for a book.** If your AI app has projects, create one for the book or for your reading in general, and add instructions such as "never spoil anything past my current page". The app keeps its memory of that book in one place.
- **Say where you are.** "So far" and "up to this page" help the assistant stay inside what you've already read.
- **Save battery.** Set an [idle timeout](settings.html) so the server and Wi-Fi turn off when you stop chatting.
