Landing page options

Three directions for the landing page. A is the one that is built out and live as the home page. B and C are mocks of the first screen only. This page and the mocks aren't linked from the site and can be deleted once a direction is settled.

  1. A. Two screens

    An e-reader and a phone side by side. Three tabs each play a short conversation, and the reader reacts: the selected word, a new highlight, a page turn with an e-ink flash.

    Good at
    Showing the whole idea in ten seconds, including that the reader and the phone are linked.
    Costs
    The most moving parts. Each conversation is a list in the page's HTML and the script is about 90 lines, so changes are edits to text.

    Open A

  2. B. Margin notes

    No devices. One page of a book, with the questions and answers written in its margin next to the passages they're about.

    Good at
    Feeling like reading. Fully static, no script, nothing to keep in sync except the text.
    Costs
    Doesn't show that it happens across two devices, or that the assistant can act on the reader.

    Open B

  3. C. The page is the reader

    The whole landing page is an e-reader screen: greyscale, dithered, and turned a page at a time with a refresh flash.

    Good at
    Being memorable to people who love e-ink. Matches the icon.
    Costs
    Paging hides content behind clicks and is worse for search engines and skimming. More of a toy than an explanation.

    Open C
