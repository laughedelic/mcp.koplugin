Read on e-ink. Talk it over on your phone.

KOReader MCP is a plugin that connects your e-reader to the AI app you already use. The assistant can see the page you're on, search the book, and save highlights and notes back into it.

Things you can ask

  • “What does this passage mean?”
  • “Summarize the chapter so far. No spoilers.”
  • “Who is this character again?”
  • “Translate the paragraph I selected.”
  • “Highlight that and add a note with what we just worked out.”
  • “Take me back to where the letter is first mentioned.”
  • “What was going on in the world when this was written?”
  • “Quiz me on what I read today.”

Why the phone, and not the reader itself

Typing on e-ink is slow

Your reader is great for reading and bad for writing. Keep the book there and have the conversation on a real keyboard.

Your chats stay in one place

The conversation happens in the AI app you already use, with its history and memory, so a talk about chapter 3 can pick up again at chapter 20.

No API keys

You add the reader to your AI app as a connector and pair it once. There is nothing to configure on the reader beyond turning the server on.

Three steps to set up

  1. Install the plugin

    Get it from KOReader’s App Store plugin, or copy the folder onto your reader.

  2. Turn on the MCP server

    It’s a checkbox in KOReader’s Tools menu. The reader then shows an address and how to sign in.

  3. Add it to your AI app

    Paste the address into Claude or Gemini as a connector, sign in, and ask about your book.

Good to know

  • Your reader has to be awake and on Wi-Fi, with the book open, while you chat.
  • Tested with Claude and Gemini. Other apps that can connect to a remote MCP server should work too.
  • By default, requests travel through a small open-source relay that passes them along and doesn’t store your books, highlights or notes. You can run your own.
  • This is early software from one developer. It’s open source, and bug reports are welcome.
